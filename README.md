**c.  In your own words, explain the difference between an exon and an intron**
 - An exon is a part of a gene that stays in the mature RNA after splicing, while an intron is a region between exons that is removed during RNA splicing.
 
**d. Describe whether the introns generally appear longer or shorter than the exons in your gene**
 - The introns in the HEXA gene generally appear longer than the individual exons. In the UCSC Genome Browser, the exon regions appear as smaller blocks, while the regions connecting them are usually longer. This shows that the HEXA gene contains relatively short exons separated by longer intron regions.

## Interpretation

The UCSC Genome Browser showed that the HEXA gene is located on chromosome 15 and covers about 35.5 kb of DNA. By looking at the gene structure, I was able to see that HEXA has multiple exons and introns, as well as different transcript models. The UCSC tracks also showed many genetic variants and regions that are conserved across different species.

The selected ClinVar variant, NM_000520.6(HEXA):c.1274_1277dup (p.Tyr427fs), is located within the HEXA gene in a coding exon. ClinVar reports this variant as Pathogenic and associates it with Tay-Sachs disease. Because it is a duplication that causes a frameshift, it can change the amino acid sequence of the HEXA protein and may affect how the protein works.

This activity helped me understand how UCSC and ClinVar can be used to study genes and genetic variants. UCSC helped me see where the variant is located in the gene, while ClinVar gave information about its disease association and clinical significance. I also learned that more evidence is needed to fully understand the effect of a genetic variant.

**2. Why is knowing the exact genomic location of a disease-associated variant useful?**
- By knowing the exact genomic location helps us understand where the variant is found within the gene. We can see if it is located in an exon, intron, or coding region. This information can help researchers understand how the variant may affect the gene and its protein.

**3. What is one limitation of predicting a variant's effect only from its genomic location?**
- One limitation is that the location of a variant does not always tell us exactly what effect it will have. A variant may be located in a coding region, but we still need more evidence to know if it changes the protein or causes disease. Clinical information and laboratory studies can help confirm its actual effect.
